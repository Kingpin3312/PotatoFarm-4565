# Diligence memo — my own money

You asked where the missing angle is. I found one that matters more than
any feature gap I could name, and two smaller ones underneath it.

---

## The one that decides whether I write a check

**As scoped today, this is a real, valuable, defensible business — and
it is not a venture-scale one.**

I checked the market rather than guess at it, and the two sources I
found disagree with each other, which is itself worth noting: one puts
registered Dubai brokerage offices at roughly 4,500, another at roughly
9,800. Individual broker counts cluster around 24,000–32,000. Both
figures are from 2026 and neither is wrong exactly — they are likely
measuring "registered" differently, and **that a market this size cannot
be pinned down from public sources is a diligence gap in itself**,
before we even get to the product.

More important than the disagreement: **the market is bimodal.** A few
firms run 300–400 agents — White & Co alone has close to 400 — and
the overwhelming majority of those four-to-ten-thousand registered
offices are one, two or three-person operations. Your own website
already says it plainly: below eight agents, this isn't worth it for
either of you.

So the real serviceable market is not four thousand firms. It's the few
hundred that clear your own floor. Run the arithmetic generously — say
500 qualifying brokerages, eighteen agents average, full capture, no
competition:

    500 x 18 agents x $70/mo x 12 = $7.56M ARR ceiling

**That is the ceiling. Not the target — the absolute maximum if you
owned the entire addressable Dubai market outright**, which you will
not, because Kendal, Goyzer and Reapit are already selling into it.
A realistic three-year outcome inside this geography alone is closer to
$1–2M ARR. That is a good business. It is not one I put growth-equity
money behind, because there is no path from there to the return a fund
needs.

**What would change my answer:** a credible expansion vector, decided
now rather than discovered later. Two real ones, not mutually exclusive.

1. **Saudi Arabia.** A materially larger market, formalising fast under
   Vision 2030, with its own compliance regime a UAE-only competitor
   will not have bothered to learn. The same unfair advantage you have
   built here — Trakheesi, DLD timelines, UAE AML — has a Saudi
   equivalent nobody has built yet either.
2. **The same architecture, a different vertical.** WhatsApp-first lead
   qualification plus a compliance backbone is not inherently a real
   estate idea. Car dealerships, insurance brokers, private school
   admissions — anything in the Gulf that is WhatsApp-heavy and
   regulator-heavy is the same product with different forms.

I would want to see which of those two is the actual plan before I
invest, not after.

---

## The risk nobody has named yet: the number does not belong to the
brokerage

Every part of this product assumes a brokerage's WhatsApp number is a
durable, brokerage-owned asset. In this specific market, agents change
brokerages constantly — commission splits move, and agents follow the
better split. Many of them talk to buyers from a **personal** WhatsApp
number, not a company one, because that number and that relationship are
theirs regardless of who they work for this year.

If that is true at any meaningful scale, it changes what you are
actually selling. You are not selling a system of record for the
brokerage's client relationships — you are selling a tool the *agent*
uses, and the agent can leave and take the relationship with them,
CRM history and all, the same way they always have.

This is not a reason not to invest. It is a question I would want
answered with real numbers before I did: **what share of enquiries in a
pilot brokerage actually arrive on a company number versus a personal
one, and does the product's retention math survive if that number is
low?** If it turns out most volume already lands on a shared company
line — which the WhatsApp Business API set-up you have built rather
implies — this may be a non-issue. But nobody has measured it, because
there has not yet been a real brokerage to measure it on.

## The domain-edge question, asked the way a good investor asks it

Not "do you know this industry" — that is a weak question and everyone
says yes. The real one: **what unfair advantage does the founding team
already have that a fund can underwrite?**

You have real operating experience in Dubai construction and fit-out —
developer relationships, Tier-1 contractor relationships, a working
understanding of how this market actually moves paperwork. That is
worth something concrete if it converts into warm introductions to
brokerage principals, and worth much less if this is being built and
sold cold into a market you are adjacent to rather than embedded in.

**Which is it?** That answer changes the first twelve months of the
plan more than any feature on the roadmap does.

---

## What I am not worried about

The compliance depth is real and it is a genuine moat — nobody else
selling into this market is building for AML, REAR or Trakheesi, and
that is not a feature a competitor bolts on in a sprint. The engineering
discipline is unusually high for this stage. None of that is the gap.

**The gap is scope, not quality.** A brilliant answer to a question small
enough that winning it completely still does not clear a venture bar.

---

## What I would want before signing

1. **A number for the personal-versus-company WhatsApp question**, from
   the pilot, not from a guess.
2. **A one-page answer on Saudi or the adjacent-vertical thesis** —
   which one, and why that one first.
3. **Named warm-intro pathway**, if one exists, made explicit rather than
   implied.

Bring me those three and the conversation changes from "interesting
regional business" to "seed cheque."
