# Viewing feedback and vendor reports

Taken from Reapit, where the mobile app logs client feedback after a
viewing. Ours collects it from the **buyer** rather than from the agent's
memory a week later, which is the difference between a report and a
recollection.

## Getting an answer at all

Feedback nobody gives is not data, so all three decisions here are about
response rate rather than data quality.

**One question, not a survey.** A four-question form gets about five
percent back. One question with four tappable answers gets most people,
and the follow-up only happens if they engage with the first.

**Two hours later, never after 8pm.** Asking as somebody walks out of a
viewing gets a polite answer rather than a true one. Two hours on they
are in the car or at home and have decided what they actually think. A
survey at 9pm is the message that makes somebody mute the thread.

**Ask like a person.** "Please rate your viewing experience from 1 to 5"
is a survey and reads like one. "What did you think?" is a question, and
the four options do the structuring without the buyer noticing they have
been structured.

Somebody who says they want to make an offer is **not** asked why. Asking
loses momentum at exactly the wrong moment.

## The report is the conversation an agent is avoiding

Vendor communication is the most common complaint about estate agents in
every market anybody has measured, and the reason is that agents have
nothing to say. "Three viewings, no news" is not a report, it is an
apology.

Telling an owner their price is wrong is the hardest conversation in this
business, and it is far easier to have with a count than with an opinion.

## Three patterns that mean different things

"It's not selling" is a useless summary because it covers three
completely different problems:

| Pattern | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Viewings, no offers** | Priced above what the market will pay. The common one, and the one nobody wants to say out loud |
| **Almost no viewings after 30 days** | Priced out of the searches buyers are running. They are not even coming to look — a different problem, and conflating it with the first wastes a month |
| **"Not as advertised" twice or more** | The listing is overselling it. Every viewing is burning goodwill and an agent's afternoon |

The third beats the other two when it appears, because fixing the photos
comes before anything else.

Seven tests cover the interpretation, including the case that matters
most: **an offer means no price signal**, however many people mentioned
the price.

## Buyer comments are never sent verbatim

Two reasons, both real. Buyers are blunt in a way that is unhelpful when
repeated to an owner about their home. And a comment identifiable to one
viewer is a privacy problem in a document going to a third party.

Counts are the honest version anyway. "Three of five viewers mentioned
the second bedroom" carries far more weight than one person saying it
rudely.

## A price recommendation needs strong evidence

Only proposed when the signal is strong — enough viewings, no offers, and
a third of viewers naming the price. Recommending a price drop on two
data points is how an agent loses an instruction.

## Not built yet

- Sending the report. It is composed and stored; nothing delivers it. The
  obvious channel is the one we already own.
- Reasons that are specific to the property. "Second bedroom too small"
  is more useful than "size", and the assistant could learn the recurring
  ones per listing.
