Trakheesi permits, and the day yours lapses

Every Dubai property advertisement needs a permit. Advertising without a valid one is an offence rather than a portal inconvenience — and the expiry is the part that catches people.

What it covers

The Trakheesi permit issued by the Dubai Land Department authorises a specific advertisement for a specific property. It has a number and it has an expiry date, and both matter.

Why expiry is the real risk

Getting a permit is a process people remember. Renewing one is not. A listing that has been live for months quietly passes its expiry, the portal pulls it or refuses an update, and the brokerage is advertising without authorisation until somebody notices.

The failure is silent in both directions. The portal does not usually tell you why, and the permit does not announce itself. It is discovered when an owner rings to ask why their villa is no longer on Bayut.

A practical routine

01

Store the expiry, not just the number

Most systems record the permit number and stop there. The date is the field that prevents the problem.

02

Warn at fourteen days

Enough time to renew without drama. Warning on the day is warning too late.

03

Block publishing without one

The cheapest control there is. If a listing cannot go out without a valid permit, the problem cannot start.

PotatoFarm.io refuses to publish without a valid permit.
